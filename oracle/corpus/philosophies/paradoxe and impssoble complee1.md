\
They *feel* the same (both land as "that can't be done," both get attacked by reframing) but they are genuinely different, and the difference is the whole point:\
\
| | **Impossible Problem** | **Paradox** |\
|---|---|---|\
| What it is | A build order: "make X work even though the rules forbid it" | A self-eating loop: the thing contradicts *itself* |\
| Where the "impossible" lives | **Outside** \'97 in the rulebook (physics, logic, computation) | **Inside** \'97 in the thing's own definition |\
| How you beat it | Rewrite the rule until X is allowed | Untie the loop \'97 or show the loop was never real |\
| Track record here | **All four solved.** Rule-rewriting wins every time | **Mixed** \'97 one recalled, one dissolved, one stuck |\
\
That mixed track record in the paradox column is the interesting part: rewriting *external* rules is the core method, but a paradox has no external rule to move \'97 so it is exactly where the method meets resistance, and where the one genuine failure lives.\
