# Your Voice

*How you actually write and speak, taken from about 260 pieces — the philosophies, the speeches, the stories, the Epics, and both songbooks. Nine readers went through them separately and kept finding the same things, which is how you know these are habits rather than accidents.*

*Nothing here is invented. Every quoted line is yours, verbatim.*

---

## The one move everything else hangs off

**You argue with someone who isn't there.** You say their objection out loud, in their words, and then you knock it down.

> And you're gonna say X. Well—

> And you think I'm joking. Which means you're a tourist. Confirmed.

> "That's not entirely how it works."
> **Exactly how it works.**

> And if you say, well, you helped out that other person —

> And you say: "Well, we have very sophisticated computers." You mean computers that were built by people using math that was already inside the box of the notation of the confirmation of the bias that they already thought of?

Five different readers found this independently, across five different kinds of writing. It's the load-bearing structure of your prose. You don't explain to a reader — you conduct an argument with one, and you take both parts.

It's also why every story you write needs at least two characters. The voice can't operate without something to push against.

---

## The rhythm

**Periods where other people use commas.** You stack short declaratives in threes and fours:

> It's going to be hard. It's going to be nearly impossible. But we said, fuck you, we're going to do it anyways.

**Sentence length swings violently.** A long spiralling run, then a two-word fragment. *"Hot sauce."* / *"Oh. Well, now you know. So."*

**The em-dash does all the structural work.** 450 across the songs, 363 across the polished set, 172 in Favorites — roughly eight per page. Its job is self-interruption:

> our ancestors — not my ancestors. Our predecessors.

**Repetition in threes, escalating rather than explaining:**

> I've checked every fucking direction. I've looked every fucking map there is.

> A little further. A little further. A little further.

> There's no more fish. *(three times, three paragraphs)*

**Endings shrink.** After a long build you go flat and short, four words and stop. Never a flourish.

> One,
> by one,
> by one,
> goodbye.

> That crazy bastard.

> Fair enough.

## Your moves, named

**Ask a question, answer it yourself, and refuse yourself comfort.**
> Do I really think that's the case? I don't. I really don't.

**Joke, then take the joke away.**
> hope you like fish, it's a joke, but you really should like fish, we have a lot of it

> Please stop having kids. It's somewhat of a joke.

**Build something lyrical, then puncture it in the very next sentence.**
> this little slice of paradise sunk to the bottom of the ocean. That sounds terrible. But it's actually, you know, true.

**Interrupt yourself at the moment of realisation.**
> Am I losing my mind I see what I best not to continue the thought.

> I know, I fucked up my words. That happens time to time.

**Deflate a panicking speaker with one word.** Someone spirals for a paragraph and the reply is *"Random."* or *"God."* or *"No."* Your readers found this is the funniest thing in your writing.

**Hedge compulsively, then override the hedge.** *"fair enough," "theoretically," "I mean," "that being said," "I don't know"* — then you go ahead and say the thing anyway.

**Concrete domestic nouns against cosmic abstraction.** Guacamole, peanut brittle, Play-Doh, a pizza in the heat, a mop, forty-seven missed calls, a beak packed with leaves. When you have an object in your hand the writing is sharp. When you only have a feeling it reaches for stock phrasing — that's the one reliable weak spot.

**Terms of address as a leash.** *my friend · tourist · jackass · motherfucker · son · captain · darling* — hung at the end of the line, intimate and usually condescending, establishing who's running the conversation.

**Drop into vernacular at the emotional peak,** never before it: *a shout that don't come back · the thread don't care what the hand intends · even if it don't satisfy.*

## Profanity — how it actually works in your writing

It's a rhythm instrument, not an insult. It lands on the stressed beat, and if you delete it the line collapses. That's the test of whether swearing is load-bearing, and yours passes.

It does three separate jobs:

**Aggression** — *"You think I'm just a janitor, motherfucker?"*

**Panic** — *"What the fuck, where the fuck, where am I now"*

**Tenderness**, which is the rarest and your best use — *"ah, such beautiful fucking words. Let's go home."*

And *"fuck you"* functions in your writing as a **positive value**. It's what people say to impossibility, not to each other:

> We're the descendants of the weirdos who said "fuck you, I'll go"

> But fuck you, we'll grow, fuck you, we'll grow

> Fuck you. I will build.

**The most controlled thing you have ever written** is who *doesn't* swear. In the Epics, the wife never curses once. The stoic Norwegian father swears exactly one time — at the precise moment he admits they are outmatched: *"Because if it were, we'd be fucked."* One reader called that the best-controlled thing in the prose, and they were right. Restraint is what makes the rest of it land.

In the Favorites songs it appears in only 5 of 21 files, always on a chorus downbeat. You already know how to ration it.

## What you never do

- **Describe anything physical.** Almost no scene-setting, no gestures, no weather.
- **Write "he said."** Dialogue runs unattributed for pages, carried by attitude alone. It works when two voices differ — one escalating, one deflating — and turns to mush when they don't.
- **Use semicolons.** Near zero, anywhere.
- **Let a grand claim stand unchallenged for a full sentence.** You hedge it, then mock your own hedge.
- **Finish loud.** You always come down before you stop.

## Where the voice changes

**Spoken (the strongest).** Everything above at full strength. This is you.

**Philosophy.** Same voice, aimed at an argument instead of a scene. Loudest register — *"Physics doesn't say no. Physics says push harder."* Still ends kind: *"We need each other."*

**Lyrics (the weakest).** When you adapt your own prose into a song you sand off what made it yours. *"You think I'm just a fucking janitor, motherfucker?"* became *"You think I'm just a janitor, motherfucker?"* — smoother, and worse. The best lines in your songbooks are the ones carried over from the prose nearly untouched.

**Technical writing.** Terse, em-dash-heavy, apposition-driven, bold for names, backticks for filenames, and a curt instruction to your future self at the end: *"Swap freely."* Recognisably the same person.

## Don't mistake these for your voice

Most of this was dictated and machine-transcribed, and the transcriber invented things. *"Shum Fettie," "the whole Nazi night," "Vegas that makes sense novelty faces for overtime," "just spags in their faces"* — that's Whisper failing, not you writing. The English translation blocks in the DaOra files are entirely the machine's voice; the same line repeated thirty times is decoder collapse, not a refrain.

But the *stumbles* are yours and worth keeping. *"I know, I fucked up my words"* stayed in the Fish speech and it's one of the best moments in it — a man breaking character to explain that his mouth runs slower than his head, then walking back into the scene without a seam.

## The line bank

Your own words, the ones that carry the most voice per inch.

> Physics doesn't say no. Physics says push harder. Physics says you didn't try hard enough. Physics says you were a coward.

> God never leaves us— and that is true, because a story can never leave you, because it was never *with* you to begin with.

> Then fucking drown.

> I dream bigger. / Fuck you. I will build.

> I solve what should exist, and then work backward to make reality catch up.

> The only place to stand and not be judged is in a grave.

> And when the universe says, "This is all there is," I do not argue. I simply answer: "Watch."

> Because I still have a job to do.

> I'm going to bet on the fucking herbs, because it's all I can do.

> It's all I have. It's all you have, it's true. So I'll bet on the herbs. What else can I do.

> when you walk out of that fucking door, you better have a goddamn smile on your face

> I've made maps because I thought there was oceans that didn't exist until I thought of them.

> You think I'm just a janitor, motherfucker? This whole place is standing 'cause of me.

> the thread didn't slip — the thread got set on fire.

> And knowing is worse than the not-knowing, oh God, the knowing is worse.

> 'Cause maybe a kind lie is the last gift I've got.

> We're the descendants of the weirdos who said "fuck you, I'll go" — and I'm grateful every goddamn day for each.

> One, by one, by one, goodbye.

> Time always needs people to write the stories. So maybe that's your place — not to make the stories, just to write them down.

> I'm not fucking your question up just as like a child to answer the question and be like, I win because I changed the rules of the game.

## If someone had to imitate you

Ask what the reader is about to object to. Say it in their mouth. Knock it down. Keep the sentences short and let the periods do the work commas would. Interrupt yourself when you notice something. Swear on the stressed beat and nowhere else. Reach for a kitchen object when the idea gets too large. Build the thing up and then knock it down yourself before anyone else can.

Then stop early, and stop quiet.
